Instructor guide · Complete journey

The complete nine-scene journey uses a 12-minute content timeline. Default playback is 1.25× (about 9 minutes 36 seconds), plus optional prediction pauses; students can choose 1×, 1.5×, or 2×. It follows the approved storyboard from a food protein through digestion, absorption, cellular protein synthesis, insulin, and other amino-acid fates.

Run & teach

Unzip the package and open index.html in a current browser, or serve the folder with a static web server. Every illustration, molecular reference, script, and caption is local. No accounts or database are required. Browser speech availability depends on the device; captions and the full descriptive transcript work without speech.

Play begins the journey. Previous, Next, and Replay move between teaching steps. Use the timeline to revisit any point. All three prediction pauses allow students to reveal or continue without a grade. Keyboard: Space toggles playback, Left/Right move between steps when focus is on the page. Focused controls retain their normal keyboard behavior.

Instructor narration

The handoff’s original narration is in narration.json and assets/narration.js. The latter is loaded by the standalone page and is the editable runtime source. Captions are sentence segments mapped across each scene. A browser voice is optional and starts only after a student enables it and presses Play. There is no prerecorded instructor audio supplied.

To use your own recordings, add local files and define window.INSTRUCTOR_AUDIO = ['audio/scene1.mp3','audio/scene2.mp3','audio/scene3.mp3','audio/scene4.mp3','audio/scene5.mp3','audio/scene6.mp3','audio/scene7.mp3','audio/scene8.mp3','audio/scene9.mp3']; in a script before app.js. Record to scene durations 60, 85, 50, 85, 55, 105, 85, 95, and 100 seconds, including pauses. Recordings seek with the visual timeline and stop during prediction pauses. Unavailable recordings fall back to browser speech. Replace sentence time segmentation with your recorded cue timings for final production.

Scientific scope

The tracked protein is hen egg-white lysozyme. Native backbone coordinates are experimental. Enlarging a short sequence, compacting the chain, hypothetical substrate binding changes, and digestion motion are conceptual teaching illustrations. The first eight residues shown are Lys–Val–Phe–Gly–Arg–Cys–Glu–Leu. Colors track side-chain classes; colors do not identify atom elements except in the enlarged chemical view.

Lysozyme is unusually resistant to denaturation and digestion because of its compact disulfide-stabilized structure. The acid and proteolysis animation illustrates the general digestive principles in the supplied narration, not measured lysozyme unfolding kinetics. Disulfide links are omitted from the backbone view and are not represented as being acid-cleaved. Proteases hydrolyze peptide bonds with water. The brush-border close-up follows the actual first Lys–Val dipeptide and free phenylalanine from the tracked lysozyme segment. Other tracked free amino acids, including glutamate, represent the broader absorbed pool. Chemical charge states are simplified and depend on pH.

The enzyme-binding sugar fragment is schematic. The altered cleft is hypothetical and does not predict a specific substitution. Sickle hemoglobin remains a distinct narration example, not a transformation of the tracked food protein.

New-scene scientific notes

Free amino acids and small peptides cross the apical membrane using different transport systems. PEPT1 co-transports H+ with di-/tripeptides; intracellular peptidases largely hydrolyze these peptides. Free amino acids exit basolaterally into portal circulation before liver and general circulation. The membrane transport routes and speeds are conceptual. The amino-acid transporter is an original schematic helix bundle; it is not labeled as a specific experimentally determined transporter.

DNA remains inside the nucleus. The double helix is an experimental shape reference, not the CALM1 gene sequence. Transcription and export are simplified; RNA processing is implied, not animated in detail. Human calmodulin is the distinct new body protein. Its codon view shows an internal segment (mature residues 8–14): Gln–Ile–Ala–Glu–Phe–Lys–Glu. The displayed synonymous codons are illustrative, not an actual CALM1 transcript. Anticodons run antiparallel to mRNA. Initiation and termination are omitted in this close-up. PDB 1CLL contains 144 resolved C-alpha positions of the 148-residue mature protein and shows one calcium-bound conformation.

The tRNA fold reference is yeast phenylalanine tRNA, used to illustrate general tRNA shape rather than claim all depicted human tRNAs have that structure. The ribosome is an experimental human 80S model. Peptide growth and folding are conceptual. The animation follows the order of an encoded body-protein segment, not the order of the eaten protein.

In a beta cell, preproinsulin enters rough ER, loses its signal peptide to become proinsulin, folds and forms disulfides, passes through Golgi, and undergoes C-peptide removal in secretory granules. Mature insulin has A and B chains linked by disulfides. The native insulin model is experimental; precursor shapes and movement are schematic. The Lys–Thr fragment in the digestion view corresponds to insulin B-chain residues 29–30. Conventional oral insulin has insufficient therapeutic bioavailability; engineered oral delivery is a separate exception. Routine insulin injections are usually subcutaneous. Bovine growth hormone also has species-specific signaling considerations; no inference about milk or puberty is made.

Fuel use distinguishes nitrogen from carbon. Transamination/deamination precedes nitrogen handling as ammonia/ammonium and urea production in the liver. The glutamate-to-keto-acid drawing illustrates oxidative deamination conceptually. Urea passes in blood to kidneys for urine excretion. Carbon skeletons can supply energy; only glucogenic amino acids can contribute to glucose. Sustained surplus energy can contribute to fat storage. There is no dedicated depot for excess amino acids or intact dietary protein. Cropped organ plates and drawn arrows are schematic physiological routes, not literal vessel connections.

Accessibility and delivery files

Captions, descriptive transcript, keyboard-operable controls and optional explanations are provided. Reduced motion removes ambient molecular rotation and bobbing; instructional transport, growth and camera progress still follow the timeline. On narrow screens, swipe the illustration horizontally to inspect details while playback controls and captions remain in the page width. Scrubbing intentionally skips prediction stops, so they are learning prompts rather than a mandatory gate.

captions.vtt contains the same sentence timing used by the visual player. Timings are proportional to sentence word count within each scene, not aligned to a recorded instructor voice. narration.json and assets/narration.js contain the original editable narration. audio/README.txt describes insertion points. The short demonstration recording is accelerated and silent; it is not a substitute for the full student tutorial.
